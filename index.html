What this simulator does

This tool models how a healthcare waitlist evolves over time based on demand, appointment capacity, non-attendance (DNA) behaviour, and patient-side dropout.

It runs a stochastic discrete-time queueing model and reports results across multiple Monte Carlo replications, so outputs reflect a distribution of plausible outcomes rather than a single trajectory.

Designed for service planning, policy analysis, and scenario testing. All calculations use synthetic data only.

How the model works

  • Daily referrals arrive as a Poisson process (mean = avg referrals/day).
  • Capacity is a fixed number of appointment slots per day, set by staffing.
  • Each booked patient leaves the queue. With probability pDNA they did-not-attend; with probability prebook they re-enter the queue after the rebook delay. Otherwise they leave the system.
  • Patients waiting in the queue can drop out independently each day at a small per-day hazard, reflecting people who move, recover, or disengage.
  • Each replication uses a deterministic seed offset from the base seed, so results are reproducible.

Interpreting outputs

  • Stable system: queue trajectory flattens; wait-time metrics are meaningful.
  • Unstable system: queue keeps growing across the horizon. Steady-state metrics are not meaningful — the system needs more capacity, lower demand, or higher attrition.
  • Confidence band: shows variation across replications, not real-world uncertainty about parameters.
  • £ / week saved: illustrative only — incremental annual cost (from extra slots/day inferred to extra WTEs) divided by the median reduction in median wait, expressed in weeks.

Limitations

  • Single-server queue: no priority tiers, no multiple clinician streams, no heterogeneous appointment lengths.
  • Poisson arrivals assume independence between days and may understate over-dispersion seen in real referral data.
  • Capacity is exogenous and constant; real capacity flexes with leave, sickness, and case-mix.
  • This is a teaching and scenario-comparison tool, not a forecasting system.
